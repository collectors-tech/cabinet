# Trust Network Roadmap Decision Matrix

This matrix is the working decision ledger for #1957. Rows are intentionally
explicit about unresolved decisions so post-beta implementation issues do not
inherit hidden contradictions from the source pack.

| Source area | Canonical rule | Rejected alternative | Unresolved decision | Security/privacy impact | Delivery phase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Protocol and technology roles | Signed Cabinet objects are the authority; transports and mirrors only move or verify those objects. | Treating Git history, Radicle identity, DHT entries, CRDT state, or peer transport as authoritative by themselves. | Exact component choice per transport/storage layer after schema approval. | Prevents a mirror or peer session from silently overriding local private state. | 4. Git/Radicle publication; 5. local P2P sessions |
| Public Git/Radicle ledgers and mirrors | Public mirrors may publish selected signed claims or hash manifests only. | Publishing private inventory rows, locations, notes, contacts, or values by default. | Mirror freshness, stale-read warnings, and retirement metadata format. | Limits privacy leakage while preserving public verification. | 4. Git/Radicle publication |
| Signed feedback vaults | Feedback is a signed object with provenance and revocation evidence; deletion does not erase prior signed proof. | Mutable unversioned feedback totals as reputation authority. | Redaction and appeal rules for abusive or legally sensitive feedback. | Keeps auditability without turning feedback logs into secret stores. | 3. signed receipts/feedback |
| Signed trade receipts and QR/manual handoff | Receipts record participant acknowledgements and external consideration notation only. | Escrow, held balances, checkout, stored value, or payment processing inside Cabinet core. | Receipt canonical serialization and participant countersignature flow. | Avoids regulated payment custody and makes transfer evidence portable. | 3. signed receipts/feedback |
| Offline transaction queues and conflicts | Offline events are idempotent signed claims with conflict evidence and manual resolution. | Assuming distributed atomic updates or making one device ledger silently authoritative. | Reservation expiry, double-trade conflict ranking, and merge UI states. | Prevents silent ownership corruption during disconnected trades. | 5. local P2P sessions |
| Direct local P2P exchange | Peer sessions exchange only explicitly selected signed objects or claims. | Ambient access to local database, file system, tools, or private collection metadata. | Platform compatibility for BLE, Wi-Fi Direct, WebRTC, libp2p, and fallback QR/manual modes. | Preserves local-first privacy and constrains peer attack surface. | 5. local P2P sessions |
| Catalogue bundle distribution | Catalogue bundles are versioned manifests with Merkle/hash verification. | Trusting a transport, hosting provider, or mutable catalogue endpoint without signed verification. | Bundle signing authority, mirror quorum, and rollback policy. | Mitigates catalogue poisoning and stale bundle attacks. | 6. catalogue distribution |
| Identity and registration | Identity keys, rotation, recovery, and revocation need one canonical suite that works without secure-enclave assumptions. | Mixing did:key, PGP/GPG/SSH anchors, and hardware-only keys as interchangeable authority. | Final key type, encoding, recovery, and compromised-key migration format. | Reduces account/key confusion and makes revocation testable. | 2. paper prototype/schema; 3. signed proof |
| Reputation and validation scoring | Identity validation, transaction confidence, and ongoing reputation are separate calculations. | Combining additive identity checks, feedback reputation, EigenTrust-style propagation, and tier tables into one score. | Formula bounds, attenuation, tier thresholds, and test vectors. | Avoids inflated trust claims and makes scoring explainable. | 2. paper prototype/schema |
| Governance and registry authority | Governance decisions must define bootstrap, quorum, revocation, appeal, and retirement before registry code. | A central whitelist that later claims to be community-owned without migration rules. | Bootstrap authority and quorum model. | Prevents opaque central control or unreviewable community capture. | 6. governance/network nodes |
| Security threats | Every approved architecture requirement maps to a threat, mitigation, test, and user-visible recovery. | Treating threat modeling as narrative documentation without executable follow-up. | Test-vector format and paper-prototype evidence storage. | Keeps fake peer/store, Sybil/eclipse, tampering, forged/deleted feedback, key compromise, poisoning, and privacy leakage visible. | 2. paper prototype/schema |
